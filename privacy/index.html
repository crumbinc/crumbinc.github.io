Privacy

Crumb privacy policy

Last updated
17 November 2026
Publisher
Crumb, Australia
Contact
appsupport@outbox.au
Applies to
Crumb for Android, version 0.5.0 and later, and the Crumb website at crumb.outbox.au
This page
https://crumb.outbox.au/privacy/

1. The short version

  • Crumb has no account, no ads and no analytics.
  • Crumb has no server of its own. We do not collect your diary, recipes, weight, photos or settings. They stay on your phone.
  • Crumb goes online only to look up products on Open Food Facts, to download product data, to fetch one web page when you give it an address, and, only if you turn on Read labels online with your own Google key, to send a label photo to Google Cloud Vision.
  • Those servers can see your phone's IP address and the app name and version, as any website can.
  • Voice search is off unless you turn it on. It uses the phone's own speech service. Crumb never records or keeps the sound.
  • Crumb has no in-app payments.
  • Crumb is for people aged 18 and over.
  • If you email us, we get your email address and what you write. That is the only personal information we hold. See section 14.

2. Who publishes Crumb

Crumb is published by Crumb, an organisation in Australia. In this policy "we" and "us" mean Crumb. You can reach us at appsupport@outbox.au.

3. The Privacy Act and the Australian Privacy Principles

The Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and its Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) bind Australian Government agencies and organisations with an annual turnover above $3 million. They also bind some smaller ones, for example health service providers and businesses that trade in personal information.

Whether or not the Act applies, we follow the APPs in plain terms:

  • APPs 1 and 5: this policy says what we do. It is free and kept up to date.
  • APPs 3 and 6: we collect only what we need (your email, if you write to us) and use it only to answer you.
  • APP 8: we say where requests from the app go, including outside Australia (section 5).
  • APP 11: we hold almost nothing, and we keep emails safe (section 15).
  • APPs 12 and 13: you can ask for the information we hold about you, and ask us to correct or delete it (section 16).

4. What stays on your phone

Crumb keeps this on your phone, in its private storage:

  • Your diary: meals, serving sizes, quick adds, weight, waist, water, caffeine and goals.
  • Your body details: date of birth, sex, height, weight, activity level and, if you choose to say so, pregnancy or breastfeeding. Crumb uses them to work out energy and nutrient targets.
  • Your recipes, recipe photos, calendar plans, cooking history, changes you made to recipes you ate, and shopping list.
  • Products you scanned, searched for, favourited or added by hand, and your search history.
  • Your settings, Diary sections and reminder times.
  • Offline product data: the Australian products that come with Crumb, plus any packs you chose to download.
  • Which version of the health and legal notice you accepted, and the date.
  • A short local crash record (section 10).
  • Your own Google Cloud Vision key, if you add one (section 5). It is locked by Android and is never in a backup.
  • Product pictures Crumb has loaded, kept as a cache so they show again without the internet.

None of this is sent to us or anyone else by Crumb, except a label photo you choose to read online (section 5). Photos you add are shrunk and re-saved without the camera's hidden data (such as GPS position or device model).

Android's Auto Backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off for Crumb. Google does not copy this data to your Google account through them.

5. What Crumb sends, to whom, and when

Crumb makes a request over the internet only in the cases below. Every request uses HTTPS, and Crumb refuses any other address.

If a download stops, Crumb asks to carry on from where it stopped.

How the update of Australian products works: Crumb asks in setup, and you can change it any time in Me, Settings, Offline food data, under "Keep products up to date". It is on unless you turn it off. While it is on and the products that come with Crumb are installed, Crumb checks about once a week in the background for new change files. By default it does this on Wi-Fi only. When it is off, Crumb schedules no check. You can also tap Update in Me, Settings, Offline food data, which works either way. It uses the same hosts as above.

How recipe import works: Crumb never does it in the background and never without your tap. It sends no cookies and no login details, only the fixed app name and version described below. It refuses addresses that point to your own network (for example a router or a home server). If the page passes you on to another public https page, Crumb follows it.

Every request to Open Food Facts and the Amazon store carries a fixed User-Agent: the app name and version, and a generic contact address that belongs to no person. No request carries your diary, history, weight, water, recipes, date of birth, settings, an account name, a device ID or an advertising ID.

Links open in your browser. After that, that website's own policy applies.

Reading labels online (Google Cloud Vision, off unless you turn it on)

This is an advanced option for people who have their own Google Cloud Vision key. It is off until you turn on Me, Settings, Read labels online, agree on the consent sheet and add your key. When it is on, the label photo you are scanning goes to Google (vision.googleapis.com), with your key, over an encrypted connection, when the phone is unsure of its reading or you tap Read again online. Nothing else is in the request: no barcode, diary, product name or settings. Google handles the photo under its Cloud Vision terms and your Google account; Google's page on how it uses Cloud Vision data is https://cloud.google.com/vision/docs/data-usage. Crumb has no server and sees neither the photo nor the result there. The app works fully without it. You can turn it off or remove the key at any time in the same place. Your key is kept on your phone, locked by Android, and is never in a backup.

6. Who can see those requests

  • Open Food Facts (the Open Food Facts association, which runs openfoodfacts.org and its subdomains), the Amazon store and any website you ask Crumb to fetch can see the IP address your phone or network is using and the app name and version. They see the barcode or search text you sent them. They may keep server logs under their own policies.
  • Google, only if you turn on Read labels online, sees the label photo and your IP address under its own terms.
  • We do not run any of these servers and see none of these logs.
  • Open Food Facts has its own terms and policy: https://world.openfoodfacts.org/legal and https://world.openfoodfacts.org/privacy
  • Some of these servers are outside Australia. The Amazon store is in Paris, France. Open Food Facts, Google and the sites you ask Crumb to fetch decide where their own servers are.

7. What Crumb does not do

  • No account, login or sign-up.
  • No ads, no ad network, no advertising ID.
  • No analytics, no tracking and no crash reporting service, and no code for them in the app.
  • No in-app payments and no billing library. Crumb never sees card or payment details and keeps none (section 9).
  • No cookies in the app.
  • No location, contacts or storage permission. The microphone permission is used only for voice search (section 8).
  • No selling of data. We have none to sell. The only data that leaves the phone is in section 5.

8. Voice search and the microphone

Voice search is off unless you turn it on in Me, Settings, Diary and trackers, Voice search. When it is on, tapping the mic asks the phone's own speech recognition to turn what you say into text, which Crumb then searches for. Crumb asks for offline recognition first, never records or keeps the sound, and sends nothing itself. If your phone has no offline speech pack, the phone's speech service may send the sound to its maker. That is the phone's service, not Crumb, and its own terms apply. You can turn voice search off at any time. Android asks for the microphone permission the first time you tap the mic. Without the permission, everything else in Crumb works.

9. Payments

Crumb has no in-app payments, tips or purchases, and every part of it is free. Crumb itself never asks for, sees or keeps card or payment details.

10. Your date of birth, children, and crash records

Date of birth and age. Crumb is made for people aged 18 and over. When you set Crumb up, it asks for your date of birth. If the date shows an age under 18, Crumb does not open and shows a message. It names Kids Helpline (1800 55 1800, any time, for ages 5 to 25) first and the Butterfly National Helpline (1800 33 4673, 8am to midnight, every day except national public holidays) second. The date is also used to work out your age for energy and nutrient targets. It stays on your phone and is never sent. The check relies on the date you type. Crumb cannot confirm it. If you are a parent or carer and a child in your care uses Crumb, uninstall it. Crumb holds no data about the child on any server of ours, so there is nothing else for us to delete.

Crash records. Crumb sends no crash reports. If it stops unexpectedly, it writes a short record on your phone: the time, the app version, the make and model of the phone, the Android version and the technical error trace. Crumb keeps the last three crashes and the last three errors that did not close the app, and replaces the oldest. You can read them in Me, Settings, About Crumb, and you choose whether to copy or share one. If you send one to us, we get what you send. A crash record leaves your phone only if you choose to include it when you report a problem.

11. Backups and exports

Crumb never uploads a backup. A backup is a file you make on purpose, or a scheduled backup to a folder you pick.

  • A backup zip holds your foods, recipes, diary, plans, goals, settings, body details (including date of birth), weight and photos.
  • A CSV export holds your diary, energy, weight, waist, water and caffeine in one zip.
  • A recipe file, PDF, Word file or QR code holds the recipe you chose to share. It goes only where you send it from the share sheet.
  • These files are not locked or encrypted. Keep them somewhere private. If the folder you chose syncs to a cloud service, that service gets a copy and its policy applies. Backups already in the folder stay there if you stop scheduled backups.

13. The camera, the label reader, Document scan and your photos

The barcode scanner and the label reader use Google ML Kit, with models built into Crumb. They run on your phone. The live camera picture is not saved or sent. A label photo is deleted as soon as it has been read. A meal or food photo is kept on your phone only if you choose to add it. The one exception is the label photo sent to Google Cloud Vision when you turn on Read labels online (section 5).

ML Kit can send its own usage data to Google. Crumb leaves out the part of ML Kit that does this.

Document scan. The optional Document button on a label scan uses the scanner from Google Play services, not a model built into Crumb. The first time you use it, Google Play services may download the scanner module, so your phone needs internet once. Crumb makes no request for it and sends nothing; Google Play services does the download under Google's own terms. The scan runs on your phone, and the page is deleted as soon as it has been read. If Google Play services or the internet is not available, Crumb's own camera and clean-up are used instead.

14. If you email us, and this website

Email. If you write to appsupport@outbox.au, we get your email address, your name if it shows, and what you write. We use it only to answer you and to fix the problem. We keep it for 12 months and then delete it. Do not send diary, health or weight data. You do not need to.

Website. The Crumb website sets no cookies, runs no analytics and loads nothing from other sites (no fonts, scripts, maps or video). The site is hosted by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc.). It may log your IP address when you visit, under its own policy. We do not see those logs.

Payments. We do not take payments on this site.

15. Keeping it safe

We hold almost nothing. What we do hold (emails) sits in Google Workspace under a password and two-step sign-in. On your phone, Crumb's data is in the app's private storage, protected by Android's app sandbox and your screen lock. Crumb has no way to lock a backup file. Keep it safe yourself.

16. How long we keep data, and deleting it

To delete your data, follow the steps at https://crumb.outbox.au/delete-data/.

  • Data on your phone. We do not hold it, so there is nothing for us to give you or delete. Crumb keeps it until you remove it. You can see, change and delete it in Crumb. To remove everything: uninstall Crumb, or clear its storage in Android settings. To remove only offline products, use Remove in Me, Settings, Offline food data.
  • Label photos and scans. Deleted from your phone as soon as they are read. A photo sent to Google Cloud Vision is kept or deleted by Google under its own terms (section 5).
  • Crash records. The last three crashes and three errors that did not close the app, replaced as new ones arrive, and gone when you uninstall.
  • Backup and export files. Delete them from where you saved them.
  • Emails to us. Kept for 12 months. Write to appsupport@outbox.au to see, correct or delete what we hold about you. We will reply within 30 days. We may need to confirm it is you.
  • Data held by others. To see or delete anything Open Food Facts, Amazon, Google or a recipe site holds about your requests, ask them. We cannot.
  • Data breach. We have no user data to lose. If we ever hold some and lose it, we will tell the people affected and, where the law asks, the OAIC.

17. Complaints

  1. Write to us first at appsupport@outbox.au. Say what happened and what you want. We will reply within 30 days.
  2. If you are not happy, you can complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC). The OAIC can only look into a complaint about an organisation the Privacy Act covers (section 3), and it usually expects you to have complained to the organisation first. You can reach the OAIC here:
    • Website: https://www.oaic.gov.au (the "Make a privacy complaint" page has an online form)
    • Phone: 1300 363 992
    • Post: GPO Box 5288, Sydney NSW 2001

18. Changes

If we change this policy, the date at the top changes and the new text applies from then. If the change is about what Crumb sends, we update the in-app Privacy page and the Data safety section of Crumb's Google Play listing in the same release.

19. Food data, estimates and health

Product data comes from Open Food Facts (a community database under the Open Database License) and plain foods from the Australian Food Composition Database (FSANZ). It can be wrong or missing. Crumb's figures are estimates. Crumb is a food diary and calculator. It is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any medical condition. Talk to a doctor or accredited practising dietitian about your health or an allergy, and read the label.
